# The Permission I Never Revoked

I gave a contractor access on a Tuesday afternoon
and told myself I would remove it, meant it, and quite soon.

The work was two weeks long at most. The statement said as much.
I made the account, added groups, and did not think to touch

the question of the ending, since the ending was implied
by everything about the job, and nothing on my side

was built to make it happen. There was no expiry set.
There was a person's memory, and mine is what you get.

The contract closed in April. Every invoice had been paid.
The laptop came back in a box. The final call was made.

Sixteen months went by. An audit walked the directory
and found the account living there with read on inventory,

on backups, on the billing tool, on two things I forgot
were ever in that group at all, which is the harder spot.

Nobody had used it once. The access logs were clean.
That is the lucky version of a thing I had not seen.

Now the case against my rule, and it is not a weak one:
access that expires on a date will cut a job half done.

A contractor at midnight with a fix that has to ship
does not need an expiry choosing then to let it slip.

So the answer is not shorter grants. It is grants that carry dates,
and somebody who gets a note a fortnight before it terminates.

What gets you is not the account. An audit finds those fine.
It is the service key beside it, sitting outside any line,

made by hand, outside the groups, with nobody's name attached,
still working, still permitted, still entirely unmatched.
