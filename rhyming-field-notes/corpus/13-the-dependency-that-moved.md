# The Dependency That Moved

I pinned my dependencies. I want that on the record first.
The lock file was committed, and I checked it, and rehearsed

the build on a clean machine to prove it came out twice the same.
It did. I wrote it in the doc. I felt quite good about my name.

What I had not considered was the base image underneath,
which named a major version and then floated free beneath.

A build in March and one in June both claimed the selfsame tag,
and both were telling me the truth, and both produced a different bag.

The library that moved was not a thing I ever named.
It arrived as a transitive of something else I claimed.

It changed a default timeout from unlimited to ten,
which broke one job at month end and no other job again.

Month end is the reason it took four months to be found.
A quarterly failure reads as weather rather than as ground.

Now the argument against me, and it holds a fair amount:
pinning every last transitive produces a long account

of things you have to lift by hand, and therefore never do,
and an old pinned library with a known hole is worse than moving through.

The honest answer is to pin, and then to schedule the lift,
and to treat a base image tag as a variable, not a gift.

What gets you is not the library. A diff will show that fast.
It is the build from eight months back that you cannot recast,

because the tag resolves today, and resolves to something new,
and the artifact you shipped to production is a thing you cannot view.
